## 1. Outcome and evidence boundary

Help a relevant executive or their research delegate find a useful explanation of a problem, recognize Vitalii's areas of work, assess the reasoning and start a conversation. Prioritize qualified conversations over undifferentiated traffic.

Two launch audiences:

1. Enterprise software teams and IT services companies that bought AI tools but have not seen the expected delivery improvement, while spending, review load or rework grows.
2. Organizations moving agentic applications into production and encountering uncontrolled actions, unclear ownership, variable quality, operational losses or unpredictable running costs.

These are the maintainer's target situations, not a claim that all AI adoption fails. The causal diagnosis must be investigated: review capacity is one possible constraint, alongside requirements, integration, environment, testing, release and organizational factors.

This pass combines the maintainer's Executive Brief, 16 qualitative web-search probes and selected primary sources. It provides **query hypotheses and observed market vocabulary**. It does not provide measured monthly search volume, CPC, keyword difficulty, rankings, buyer interviews or this site's Search Console data. Search-result titles are evidence that publishers use a phrase, not evidence of how often customers type it. P0/P1/P2 below mean business relevance and sequencing, not estimated traffic or ranking ease. The sample is not a reproducible Google ranking audit for a specified country/device.

Working language is English. Geographic focus is not yet specified: use clear international English; do not add unsupported location pages or geographical service claims. Research terminology stays accurate even where another phrase might attract more clicks.

## 5. Outlines for the two later guides

These are suggested editorial briefs under a separate follow-up task. They do not block first launch or silently replace the PMDay article in [#2](https://github.com/oborskyivitalii/oborskyivitalii/issues/2). Write the PMDay article next as previously intended; then decide whether its accepted scope already answers one guide's question. Reuse one coherent publication when appropriate, rather than publishing competing near-duplicates.

### A. Why AI adoption can increase output without improving software delivery

- Start with the recognizable situation: broad tool access and more generated output, but disappointing delivery outcomes or rising costs.
- Distinguish local task speed, flow through the whole delivery system, business value and total operating cost.
- Inspect alternative explanations: requirements, review capacity, testing, integration, release, rework, knowledge/ownership and whether the use case matters. Do not announce the bottleneck before observing the system.
- Provide a small diagnostic table: what changed; what evidence would support a hypothesis; what might disconfirm it; what decision follows. Useful signals include queue age, review load, rework and incidents, interpreted in context. Generated lines or tool-seat usage alone are not the outcome.
- Explain the generation/understanding gap as the author's research lens; separate thesis from external evidence. Link Subprime and Externalization.
- Explain what an assessment or workshop could examine and produce. No universal recipe or promised percentage gain.
- End with the common CTA. Add short, visible answers to real questions, such as “How should we assess AI coding-tool ROI?” and “What if code review is not our bottleneck?”

### B. AI agents in production: governance, ownership and control

- Start with the gap between a compelling demo and an application acting in a changing production environment.
- Explain failure categories: incomplete task boundaries, unintended tool actions, uncertain outputs, absent evidence for release, loops/cost growth, unclear escalation and human overload. These are diagnostic categories, not a claim about a measured failure rate.
- Describe which decisions need an owner: allowed actions, evidence thresholds, evaluation, budget/time limits, stop/recovery paths, incident response and learning after incidents.
- Connect technical controls to staffing, authority and response capacity; a “human in the loop” label does not specify those responsibilities.
- Show one explicitly conceptual scenario with assumptions and limits, or an approved case if one becomes available. Do not fabricate client outcomes.
- Explain the UA research connection and remaining uncertainties; link Thinking Systems and Agentic Loops.
- Offer a scoped review of architecture, control boundaries and the operating model. End with the common CTA and visible questions about release readiness, behavior and cost ownership.

No minimum word-count target, automated keyword density, FAQ rich-result promise or bulk article generation. A short complete explanation is preferable to padding. If a guide needs new research claims, apply the relevant repository's input/review process before publishing them.

## 7. Technical SEO and AI-search treatment

- Put the core argument and links in static HTML. The decorative Canvas must not contain the only version of a heading, explanation or navigation.
- Make each page's title, description and H1/H2 hierarchy describe that page. Write natural prose; do not repeat every query variant.

- Keep existing truthful Person/ProfilePage, publication and list markup aligned with visible content. Do not invent reviews, organization affiliations, services delivered or certification schema. Do not mark an external link card as if the full article is hosted here.
- After the stable release URL is selected in #8, set consistent absolute canonicals, social URLs/images and sitemap entries; inspect deployed status, robots directives and indexing eligibility. - Verify the public site in Search Console when the owner can authorize verification. Use URL inspection/indexing reports and sitemap feedback. No paid SEO service or new analytics SDK is required for launch.
- Medium/LinkedIn originals remain evidence and distribution routes. A list of their titles is not a substitute for original useful explanations on this domain. If full editions are later republished, handle rights and canonical ownership in the publication workflow.

Do not add `llms.txt`, a new site framework, hidden AI-oriented prose or a special “GEO score” as a launch dependency. No promise of indexing, ranking or answer-engine citation follows from technical correctness.

## 8. Validation and methods of search

### Before launch

Use this inventory to interview or informally ask a few relevant readers: what did they actually search after their last disappointing AI initiative, what did they ask colleagues, and which wording matches their organization? Record answers as new evidence; no outreach is performed by this plan. Ask a reader to identify the problem, relevant research, plausible help and contact route from the page.

If the maintainer later supplies access, Keyword Planner or a keyword research service can estimate country/language-specific volume and competition. Keep estimates and date/market separate from real site data. Google Trends can help compare broad interest but is not absolute demand or reliable evidence for every narrow phrase. Do not buy subscriptions or configure campaigns as part of this task.

### After a public indexed version exists

1. Record the actual URL, publication date, submitted sitemap and indexability status under #8.
2. Review query/page impressions and clicks in Search Console. Separate author/research-name queries from problem/solution queries and group the latter by the families above. Respect reporting omissions; do not claim every visitor's query is visible.
3. Inspect qualified conversations alongside visibility: which problem, organization fit, how the person found the work and what they needed. A contact click is not a confirmed booking, qualified lead or revenue.
4. At a manual checkpoint around 4–6 weeks after indexing, revise headings, summaries and the next content priority using available evidence. This is a review point, not a traffic forecast or a scheduled automation. Small samples may require a longer observation period.
5. Use a small repeatable set of non-branded scenario prompts in conversational search as an exploratory citation check. Record date, product/model where exposed, locale, prompt and linked sources. One result is not a stable ranking; do not report a universal “AI visibility score.”

Example conversational-search prompts (hypotheses):

- “We gave every engineer an AI coding assistant. PR volume rose, but releases are not faster and senior reviewers are overloaded. What should our CTO investigate?”
- “Our AI agent passes demos but takes unexpected actions in production. How should we define ownership, controls and release readiness?”
- “We run a software services company. AI adoption raises tool and review costs, while clients expect lower prices. How should we assess the delivery model?”
- “Who works on AI-assisted SDLC governance and operating models for agentic systems?”

No client information needs to be submitted to a search service for these generic checks. Optional future measurement of contact clicks should remain lightweight and follow the chosen privacy setup; analytics/CRM integration is not required for the first launch.
