## Finding dispositions in the prepared candidate

### F01 — the delivery pilot now requires an economic result

Decision A retains its change-to-release, p90 review-age, escaped-defect, sufficient-observation and recovery conditions. Its evidence cell now additionally says:

> Compare useful release outcomes and full delivery cost with the baseline, including transition and ongoing costs; require a predeclared acceptable economic gain before expansion.

Its failure action distinguishes delivery-limit failure, economic failure and insufficient evidence:

> Cap intake if delivery limits fail. Do not expand if the economic criterion fails; revise the adoption approach or retain the baseline. Keep the pilot bounded if evidence is insufficient. Faster delivery alone does not establish value.

The editorial counterexample “faster, acceptable quality/recovery, but economically worse” can no longer satisfy the stated expansion condition. Favourable economics does not override delivery limits; insufficient evidence keeps the pilot bounded. These are checks of the proposed decision rule, not measured pilot outcomes or automated proof of business value. No universal ROI percentage or sample size is introduced. Runtime-delegation conditions remain separate.

### F02 — local repetition removed without combining the two plans

The transition to the pilot tables now begins:

> These different plans need different conditions for continuation and expansion.

It proceeds directly to intended benefit, full cost, acceptable risk and evidence that would change the decision. Customer-accountability wording is consolidated without dropping the vendor/adopter boundary, explicit bounded experimentation or either core sentence:

> Their confidence is not your risk acceptance.

> You do not owe a vendor your organisation as an experiment. You owe your customers a system you can operate.

Separate budget, risk, staffing and ownership consequences for coding-tool adoption and runtime model judgment remain intact.

The revised paragraph evaluates each proposed operating model in its context, retaining limited or no AI as valid outcomes. Part I's family of context-dependent operating models remains byte-identical; no industry-wide convergence prediction is added.

Decision A is on page 15; Decision B's complete four-row table now fits on page 16, avoiding the old split.
