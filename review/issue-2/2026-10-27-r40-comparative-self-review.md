## Findings and dispositions — recommendations only

### F01 — important: Decision A does not carry the economic claim into its admission criterion

Location: Part III, PDF pages 13–15, especially Decision A — the delivery-system pilot.

The prose requires useful outcomes, baseline comparison and full costs. Decision A's sole preserved row checks median change-to-release time, p90 review age, escaped defects, adequate observations and recovery rehearsal, but has no economic outcome condition. The full-cost/simplest-safe-alternative criterion is now under Decision B's Expand the pilot row. Thus Decision A can pass operationally while the organisation's economics worsen. This is a local inconsistency between argument and practical guide, not a claim that the essay ignores ROI everywhere.

Recommendation: before expanding the delivery-system pilot, require a predeclared acceptable comparison of useful outcomes and full transition/ongoing costs against the baseline. Do not invent a universal ROI threshold or require a particular causal-study design. Passing runtime-product economics cannot substitute for this decision.

Closure observation: a hypothetical faster-but-economically-worse delivery outcome cannot pass Decision A merely on speed/quality/recovery metrics. This gap was inherited in the old row; splitting the table made its consequence clearer. Verbatim preservation is not sufficient semantic acceptance.

### F02 — medium: the expanded final chapter sometimes repeats premises instead of advancing decisions

Location: Part III pages 13–17, especially the start of Separate decisions need separate evidence and the first two customer-accountability paragraphs.

The parallel budget/risk/staffing treatments are necessary and should remain separate. However, the later 'first investment decision' restarts the chapter's framing after both plans have been explained. The vendor/adopter exposure difference is also restated several times before the closing sentence. This weakens pace and produces multiple endings.

Recommendation: use the evidence subsection as a direct bridge from two plans to two expansion/stop conditions, not another introduction. Consolidate overlapping customer-risk prose while preserving the exact risk-acceptance and organisation-as-experiment sentences. No target word count, fixed cut percentage or restored cap is proposed.

Closure observation: each paragraph adds a decision, dependency or consequence rather than only restating a premise. Keep the newly restored substance.

### F03 — medium: the universal-balance question is not a sufficient reason about a specific investment

Location: Separate decisions need separate evidence, page 15: 'Would you still bet all-in on AI knowing that no universal balance may emerge?'

A lack of a universal industry operating model does not by itself make a particular tested application uneconomic. The question shifts back to undifferentiated 'all-in on AI' after the essay has separated two decisions. It is rhetorically forceful but has a weak link to the project-specific evidence argument.

Recommendation: keep the family-of-operating-models argument in Part I. In Part III, ground investment in the selected model's evidence, full costs and conditions for reconsideration. If retaining 'all-in', define it as imposing one model across unlike contexts, not any substantial investment. This is inherited from R38/R39, not a newly introduced R40 defect.

### F04 — minor: PDF grouping can better preserve local reading context

Locations: pages 10–11 and 15–16. Design for real review capacity starts near the bottom of page 10 with its attractive assumption; What if not? and the challenge continue on page 11. Decision B continues on page 16 with generic column headers but no repeated pilot label.

Recommendation: keep heading/assumption/refrain/first-answer grouping where practical, and label the table continuation. These are not missing-content or broken-navigation findings. Do not shrink text or remove argument to fix pagination. Table type is approximately 9 pt versus 10.6 pt for body text; actual physical-device usability was not tested.

### Optional wording, not blockers

'How must our delivery system change' slightly anticipates the answer before testing whether change is needed; subsequent conditionals already correct this, so it is not a universal-diagnosis claim. 'What, if anything, must change' would align more tightly with the engineering loop. 'Not every application needs a professor' reads like a reply to an earlier conversation; proportional modelling/expertise can be stated without the academic title. The essay does not need a complete mathematical model or a new original empirical study to fulfil its stated purpose.
