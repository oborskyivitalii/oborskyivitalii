# AI Changes the Delivery System and the Product Itself

AI enters software in two places: as a tool used to build it, and as model judgment inside the running product. Those changes need different evidence and decisions.

AI-written code can implement an ordinary application with explicitly specified behavior. A human-written application can depend on probabilistic model judgment at runtime. When both changes occur, leaders need to examine delivery flow and product behavior separately, then connect the work.

## How we build: follow the whole flow

Faster code generation does not automatically accelerate review, testing, integration or operation. If incoming work exceeds downstream capacity, the queue can grow without a corresponding increase in useful releases. If coding was the constraint, the constraint may move.

This is a conditional risk mechanism. A team may gain throughput, reduce effort or free capacity. AI may improve review and comprehension too. The practical question is what happens to this team's complete flow for comparable work.

Look at review age, work in progress, lead time, rework and stability alongside delivered value. Include ownership and recovery: can the team diagnose and restore an important component when its AI tool cannot produce a satisfactory repair?

Small batches, intake limits, stronger specifications, documentation and AI-assisted review are candidates to test against a baseline. They need evidence that they improve delivery without shifting cost into rework or team overload.

## What we build: specify behavior and authority

The product changes when a consequential runtime responsibility depends partly on probabilistic model judgment. Uncertainty Architecture calls this a **Thinking System**. The category describes an engineering condition; it does not imply consciousness, adequate control or production readiness.

Use the PMDay talk's hypothetical access-recovery assistant. It may explain approved guidance in different words, clarify missing context or escalate to support. It may not invent recovery steps, disclose someone else's data or change access without permission.

Requirements must describe acceptable variation, approved sources, required meaning, prohibited outcomes and the response to insufficient information. A prompt influences behavior. Permissions and data boundaries still need concrete enforcement.

## Evidence and control belong in the commitment

A curated reference set probes important scenarios. Its pass rate does not automatically estimate production error frequency. Evaluators can miss failures or reject useful answers. Keep versions, coverage and uncertainty attached to the evidence, and distinguish severity from average scores. A strong aggregate score does not authorize a prohibited disclosure.

Semantic checks and permissions answer different questions. Access boundaries apply before data enters model context as well as before disclosure or execution. Observe delivered results, rejection, fallback and control health. Give an authorized owner a real path to stop, narrow or change operation, then verify the intervention's effect.

Human response requires evidence, competence, decision rights, time, capacity and backup. Feedback cannot undo an irreversible disclosure. If required control is infeasible, too slow or destroys the business case, narrow, redesign or reject that AI path.

## Connect the decisions

Product criteria, evaluation, controls, release authority and service ownership are different responsibilities. Existing people can combine them. Project managers connect the required decisions, evidence and capabilities in the delivery plan without replacing technical or risk authority.

Start with one workflow: a useful outcome, approved limits, evidence, decision owners and a rehearsed fallback. Reassess when the basis changes, then decide whether to continue, narrow or stop.
