- Proposed audience: enterprise delivery leaders, product owners, project managers and engineering leaders making decisions about AI-assisted delivery or model-enabled products.
- Proposed language: English, matching the site interface and current authoring default. This is an editorial candidate, not a claim of a confirmed translation of the spoken talk. The supplied recording remains labeled **PMDay 2026 · Recording in Ukrainian**.
- Purpose: explain the talk's two independent but connected changes, then show how they change concrete requirements, evidence, release and operational responsibilities.
- Target: 1,200–1,800 useful English words. Plain continuous prose; practical decisions before named frameworks. No universal productivity effect, invented implementation case, adoption claim or service promise.

## Figure 1: an example control path

Master: `site/assets/publications/pmday-two-shifts/control-path.svg`. Raster rendition: `control-path.png`. Original vector artwork prepared for this explanatory article; no deck artwork, empirical chart or third-party image is copied.

The diagram explains the illustrative access-recovery assistant in deck slide 12. Top row: model proposal, fallible semantic check, permissions/required approval, permitted result. Rejection or an unavailable required check reaches denial/safe fallback. Both delivered and fallback paths produce evidence. An authorized decision invokes a real intervention; the effect is observed, and the next operating state is reassessed. Approved limits apply throughout; the diagram does not make those limits enforce themselves. Input/retrieval access checks are required by the example and are stated in prose, not hidden behind output-only checking.

Caption must identify a schematic example, distinguish semantic checking from authority, cover fallback outcomes and control health, and state that a closed loop is not a correctness guarantee. Human approval is required only where the approved design says so. The figure is not a universal prescribed pipeline or a measured probability chart.
